# Decision 0004: explicit action, pricing and horizon choices

Status: accepted product choices from Mike, 28 September 2026.

1. **Horizon across a clock change.** The seven-day study is a fixed 336 UTC half-hour slots starting at London local midnight of the study start date; warm-up slots are fixed 30-minute UTC slots counted back from that instant (48 per warm-up day). A London offset change inside the span is allowed. "Day d" for trips, connections and weather is the London calendar date, with local clock times converted at that date's actual offset; a change date has 46 or 50 local half-hours and daily rules (such as an 18:00 London action slot) are applied per local date. This replaces the rejection of offset changes for explicit modes; legacy modes keep rejecting.
2. **Wholesale action.** The selected action is an illustrative authorised import ceiling, not an exact requested kW. It limits home import and never forces it.
3. **Destination public charging trigger.** An EV uses destination public charging only when its next trip would otherwise be infeasible. No separate SoC threshold is introduced.
4. **Pricing the action outcome.** Public charging is priced at an illustrative **£0.79/kWh**, labelled as an editable assumption, not a tariff claim. Energy not recovered by the end of the horizon is valued at the same illustrative public rate, on the basis that the driver would buy it back at a public charger. The cost table shows the home-import cost, the public-charge cost, the unrecovered-energy value and their illustrative total separately. None of these is a settlement, bid or Axle-cash amount (see decision 0003).
5. **Not-recovered flag.** `energy_not_recovered` trips when the selected path ends with lower battery stock, more public import or more unserved travel than the normal path, beyond a 1e-6 kWh tolerance.
6. **Scenario comparison.** Scenario variants run on matched random futures (the same seed and sampling order) by default. Saved scenarios stay in the viewer's session; JSON export is out of scope for now.
7. **Legacy delivery wording.** Non-explicit modes without a service-delivery result must not claim "available delivery headroom"; the Parameters wording is corrected for those modes too.
8. **Per-EV action eligibility.** The feasibility screen is applied per EV in the planning world: the authorised import ceiling applies only to EVs whose deferred energy is recovered by day end; other EVs keep the normal path. This replaces the fleet-wide rejection that selected no action at 1,000 EVs.
9. **Default model.** The explicit wholesale action model is the default in the app; the explicit no-action model stays selectable.
10. **Individual view for action runs.** The single-EV replay gains ceiling and public-charge support so the Individual view shows action runs. Until that lands, the view shows a specific "unavailable for action runs" message, never a replay that omits the action.
11. **Editable public and price assumptions.** The public-charge and price assumptions, including the £0.79/kWh rate, are editable in Parameters with the same validation, draft-preview and stale-result rules as other assumptions.
12. **Paired difference band.** The model outputs selected-minus-normal P10/P50/P90 bands computed from per-world differences (world-first), and the Fleet view charts them. The UI never subtracts percentiles.
13. **Unserved travel and tolerance.** Unserved travel trips the not-recovered flag and is valued at the same illustrative public rate (the driver would need a public charge to make that trip); it is included in the illustrative total, because unpriced unserved travel leaves energy in the battery and would show a false saving. The unrecovered-energy and unserved-travel values use the same 1e-6 kWh tolerance as the flag. Battery-side energy is valued at the grid-side public rate; this simplification is stated in the docstring.
14. **Sampling libraries.** Random draws stay on one `np.random.default_rng(seed)` per run, passed through. Independent draws (mileage, clocks, availability, weather offsets) use `scipy.stats` distributions with `random_state=rng`, so each distribution and its parameters read as named objects. Autocorrelated time series use statsmodels: synthetic price noise is an illustrative AR(1) process from `statsmodels.tsa.arima_process.ArmaProcess.generate_sample`, fed by the same generator, with the AR coefficient stated in the assumptions module. Plain NumPy stays for array arithmetic.
15. **Code organisation.** One `model/assumptions.py` states every active assumption (value, unit, provenance, meaning). Sampling code lives together, physical calculations together, action and cost calculations together, each with module docstrings and commented equations. Superseded modes are removed unless a recorded requirement needs them.
16. **Brief first.** The brief's literal asks are finished before further mechanism build-out: the plug-in timing and plug-in SoC distribution charts are mounted for the default model, and `scripts/run_archetypes.py` runs the simulator with preset archetype parameters (for example "commutes every weekday" and "weekend long trips") and prints or plots population-level results. Workspaces with no implemented mechanism show an honest "not modelled" message rather than stubs that look finished.
17. **Notebooks run for real.** Notebooks are part of the deliverable and must execute top to bottom in the project environment: `jupyter`/`ipykernel` join the dev group with a project kernel, the research notebook's PyMC/ArviZ path runs (a `research` dependency group), and notebooks call the current explicit model APIs, not superseded modes.
18. **Remove superseded modes.** The cohort, compact (including compact DFS) and synthetic six-route one-day models are removed with their views, tests and configs. They use connection-clock-as-travel physics that M06 rejected. This explicitly supersedes decision 0001's requirement that the dashboard shows all six commercial mechanisms, M09's daily synthetic DFS case, and U04's "one-day models remain selectable references". Mechanisms without an explicit-path model are shown as "not modelled" and may return later as explicit-path candidates. Only the explicit action model (default) and explicit no-action model remain.
19. **Lenses.** Lenses that can never be available for the explicit models are retired rather than shown as stubs.
20. **Individual replay.** The Individual view replays one EV by running the vectorised kernel on a one-EV slice, so it gets the ceiling and public charging for free; the scalar replay moves to tests as a parity check. The replay also yields the plug-event table M02/U16 ask for.
21. **Assumption values live in Python.** `model/assumptions.py` holds every value with unit, evidence kind, source and meaning; the explicit JSON configs and their loaders are removed. Where two sources disagree (for example home AC limit 7.0 vs 7.2 kW), the value traced in `docs/sources/cohort-crosswalk.md` wins and the choice is noted.
22. **Dashboard design.** `docs/DASHBOARD_DESIGN.md` is the accepted layout: six top-bar pages (Overview, Drivers, Wholesale action, Scenarios, Parameters, Methods & notes), Run/Reset/model selector in a top bar on every page with six worded run states. Overview shows spread across EVs (the brief's sketch); other charts show spread across simulated weeks; every chart names which. Navigation moves to the top bar so Run works at 390 px. One EV defaults to one representative week (partly reversing M05's median default). Teal marks the selected action path everywhere.
23. **Code that explains its reasoning.** Mike must be able to walk an interviewer through the code. Comments state *why* as well as what: the modelling choice, the alternative rejected, the invariant protected, and the decision or source it follows (e.g. "decision 0004 item 8"). Each model module opens with a short "what this owns and how it fits" docstring. `docs/CODE_GUIDE.md` gives the reading order through the code, from assumptions to sampling, physics, action, aggregation and the dashboard.
24. **Explainers.** Two plain-English explainers, also shown in the app's Methods & notes page: `docs/explainers/how-it-was-built.md` (process, architecture, key decisions and why, how agents wrote, reviewed and integrated work, what was verified) and `docs/explainers/how-the-forecast-works.md` (how the fleet energy balance is simulated half-hour by half-hour, what is sampled and how, how Monte Carlo worlds are aggregated, how the wholesale action is chosen and evaluated, and what the outputs mean and do not mean). Both reference real file and function names and are checked against the code before release.
25. **Unshifted EVs.** An EV-day whose import the deferral does not shift in the planning world gets no ceiling in any evaluation world. Applying it would change evaluation outcomes for EVs that were never screened, so the cautious reading of item 8 applies. The runner reports shifted, eligible and ineligible counts per London date.
26. **Charts fully visible.** Every chart has an explicit height suited to its content (time series at least 380 px on desktop, small multiples at least 220 px each, histograms at least 320 px), uses the full container width, and keeps legends, axis titles and annotations inside the plot area without overlap. At 390 px wide, charts stack one per row, legends move below the plot, and nothing is clipped or squashed. Chart checks include a visual pass at 1440 and 390 px.
27. **Chart conventions.** Time axes show London local time, with UTC kept in hover text and tables. The standard centre line is the median (P50) with a P10–P90 band, named in every legend. Normal path is a lighter solid line; the selected path is teal; bands use about 0.3 opacity.
28. **Streamlined navigation (amends item 22).** Five pages: Overview · Drivers · Wholesale action · Compare · How it works. Parameters becomes an "Edit assumptions" dialog opened from the run bar (read-only assumptions table under How it works); every run is kept automatically (last three) for Compare; run states show as one worded chip, with a banner only for a failed run. Fallback if the dialog is awkward in the browser: the same editor as an editable How it works lens. See `docs/DASHBOARD_DESIGN.md` "Streamlining pass".
29. **Plotly for all charts.** Every chart in the app, scripts and notebooks uses Plotly, styled through the shared chart template in `ui/style.py`. No matplotlib, Altair or `st.*_chart` built-ins. Notebooks pair each chart with a short text table so they still read where Plotly does not render.
30. **Action window is one half-hour.** The illustrative action caps home import at 0 kW in the 18:00–18:30 London half-hour each study day (`defer_daily_1800_v1`), as reviewed. Design text that says 18:00–21:00 is corrected; widening the window would be a new candidate action needing its own review.
31. **Stranded EVs count their missed trips.** Once an EV is stranded, every later trip it would have made in the horizon counts as unserved travel (and is valued per item 13), so stranding cannot hide unmet travel. A charging taper is not modelled; the model contract says so.
32. **No stranding: threshold top-ups (supersedes items 3 and 31).** Mike, 28 September 2026: EVs never strand. Whenever a trip would take an EV's battery below a top-up threshold, it tops up at a public charger first, up to a top-up target, and then continues. Illustrative defaults, editable in Edit assumptions: threshold 20 % SoC, target 80 % SoC; a public charger is always available for these top-ups (the destination availability probability no longer gates them). The top-up energy is recorded as public grid import in the slot where it happens, divided by the public charging efficiency; the time spent charging is not modelled (stated as a limitation). Unserved travel is therefore zero by construction and stays in results only as a check. Public top-up cost and the not-recovered flag (items 4, 5, 13) are unchanged; the action screen (item 8) still excludes an EV-day whose deferral adds public charging.
33. **No size or runtime gates.** Mike, 28 September 2026: notebook file size and full-run time (including over 60 s at 1,000 EVs × 100 worlds) are not acceptance gates. Runtime and peak memory are still measured and reported, and memory must stay practical for a local run.
34. **One home charging power.** The separate vehicle AC limit is dropped. Home charging power is the per-cohort home charger power (7.0 kW, source-traced); the kernel's min(home charger, vehicle AC limit) becomes the home charger power alone. Removed from assumptions and the Edit assumptions dialog when the assumptions module is wired in.
35. **Compare works from slim run records.** Run history keeps, for every kept run (not only the latest), the frames Compare needs: `fleet_world_intervals`, `plug_in_world_kpis`, `cost_effect` (action runs), `weekly_bands`, the settings snapshot, seed, model and world count. `compare_runs` takes these slim records, so comparing the previous and latest run never needs a full stored result. The latest run also keeps its full result for the other pages.
36. **Settled start.** Runs start from a settled state so the reported week is not dominated by start-up catch-up: opening SoC equals the preferred target (80 %) and the warm-up is three days. Evidence: with 55 % opening SoC and one warm-up day, cutting home charging power from 7.0 to 3.6 kW raised study-week home import by about 190 kWh because warm-up refill spilled into the week; with a settled start the same cut lowers home import by 400–560 kWh and moves about 500 kWh to public top-ups. Seeded test values change with the longer warm-up. A sensitivity test checks that lower home charging power never raises study-week home import.
37. **Top-up threshold 10 %.** Mike, 28 September 2026: the default public top-up threshold is 10 % SoC (target stays 80 %), replacing item 32's 20 % default. It lets plug-ins between 10 % and 20 % SoC happen, closer to CNZ's observation that under 10 % of plug-ins start below 20 %. It stays editable in Edit assumptions (bounds 5–50 %).
38. **Price-optimised smart charging replaces the 18:00 cap (supersedes items 2, 8, 25, 30 for the action).** Mike, 28 September 2026. The selected path is smart home charging: at each plug-in, knowing the EV's SoC at plug-in, the plug-in time, its expected departure (the cohort's typical departure clock time minus a one-hour safety margin, option (a)) and the forecast prices available at decision time, the charger plans to deliver just enough energy to reach the preferred target SoC by the expected departure, using the cheapest half-hours in that window (fill cheapest first, limited by home charging power; this is optimal for a linear price with a power cap). If the window cannot deliver the need, it charges as soon as possible. The normal path is unchanged: charge as soon as plugged in. Evaluation runs the plan against each simulated week's actual departure and realised prices; an earlier-than-expected departure leaves the EV short, which shows up as public top-ups or energy not recovered and is priced (items 4, 5, 13). The illustrative cost is selected minus normal per week, as before. The single 18:00–18:30 candidate, per-EV eligibility screening and the fleet-level select-or-not decision are removed.
39. **Fleet week shows spread across EVs.** Mike, 28 September 2026: the Fleet week bands across simulated weeks are thinner than the line (a 1,000-EV fleet average moves under 1 point between weeks: plugged-in P90−P10 median 0.8 pp, SoC 0.6 pp), so they tell the viewer nothing. Fleet week's main bands show variation across EVs for each half-hour instead: SoC P10–P90 across EVs (median width about 19 points), home import per EV P10–P90 across EVs, and plugged-in share as one line per cohort plus the fleet total. Each caption states the spread shown and notes the week-to-week spread of the fleet average. Band fills are made clearly visible. The model adds a per-half-hour across-EV band frame (median across simulated weeks of each slot's across-EV quantiles).
40. **P10–P90 across weeks always shown (amends item 39).** Mike, 28 September 2026: every time-series chart always shows the P10–P90 band across simulated weeks (on by default, no toggle, including One EV). Fleet week shows both: the across-weeks band (always, even when thin, because a thin band is itself the finding) and, as a separate lighter band, P10–P90 across EVs; each is named in the legend and caption. One EV shows only the across-weeks band (it is one car). One EV's grey background strips (plugged in at home in the chosen week) get a legend entry or caption key.
41. **Realistic plug-in behaviour.** Mike, 28 September 2026 (option A). Home plug-in is no longer "every night" for most cohorts: (1) base plug-in probability below 1 for the main cohorts, from CNZ plug-ins per EV per week where the sources support it (docs/sources/cohort-crosswalk.md, CNZ report), otherwise a clearly labelled illustrative value (about 0.6 for Average UK); (2) the plug-in choice depends on SoC at arrival: always plug in when SoC is below an editable threshold (illustrative default 50 %), otherwise plug in with the base probability; (3) separate weekday and weekend home connection windows per cohort. The drawn uniform for each (world, day, EV) is taken whether or not it is used, so common random numbers hold. Target: the median plug-in SoC and plug-ins per week move toward CNZ context (52 %; share below 20 % under 10 %), reported against CNZ as context, not calibrated to it. Weather is unchanged (daily Normal around invented bases, one temperature per day for the fleet, driving efficiency only), stated as a limitation.
42. **Plug-in behaviour follows the sources (amends item 41).** Source research, 28 September 2026: the per-cohort plug-in probabilities (1.0/day; 0.2/day for infrequent charging) are supplied by the Axle brief's archetype spreadsheet ('Source archetypes'!G6:G11) and corroborated by the CNZ "Learning from Intelligent Octopus" report (May 2022, p.11 Table 2, p.19–20: most vehicles plug in daily); CNZ gives no evidence that plug-in propensity depends on SoC (habitual; range anxiety flagged as unconfirmed). So the probabilities stay as supplied and no SoC-dependent trigger is added. The one change kept from item 41: separate weekday and weekend home connection windows for Average UK and Intelligent Octopus from CNZ p.11 Fig.4 and p.12 Fig.5 (weekday arrival about 18:00, departure 07:00–08:00; weekend arrival about 17:00, departure 09:00–11:00 with a wider spread), labelled as an illustrative adaptation of the figures; the other four cohorts keep flat windows (no source). The model's median plug-in SoC remaining above CNZ's 52 % is reported plainly with its likely causes (daily plug-in and an 80 % preferred target), not tuned away.
43. **Dashboard tells the flexibility story.** Mike, 28 September 2026. The dashboard answers Axle's question: how much EV charging can be moved, when, with what confidence, what it is worth, and at what cost to drivers. Story order: who the drivers are → when they plug in and at what SoC → how much charging is available and movable → what smart charging does with it → value and driver risk. New model metrics per half-hour: flexible power available (plugged-in EVs × home charging power, kW), movable energy (kWh still needed before expected departure) and time slack (hours plugged in minus hours needed to reach target), each P10–P90 across weeks (item 40). 44. **Weekend dwell (supports item 42).** Trip dwell at the destination is an illustrative value, not supplied. 45. **Goal-review fixes (Mike, 28 September 2026).** All P1, P2 and P3 actions in `docs/reviews/2026-09-28-goal-review.md` are implemented. Two choices: (a) the published build notes are updated from the 18:00 cap to smart charging, keeping Mike's wording and voice; (b) smart charging's herding onto one cheap half-hour (about 982 kW at 05:30 against a 537 kW evening peak for 200 EVs) is labelled as a finding on Response and in Limits, and an optional fleet charging cap per half-hour is added as an editable illustrative assumption (default off). The not-recovered indicator shows magnitude (unrecovered kWh as a share of weekly home import and EV-sessions affected) with a material threshold, instead of a fleet-wide yes/no.
46. **Flexibility definitions.** Time slack counts only plugged-in EVs still below target (an EV at target has nothing to move) and carries a P10–P90 band across weeks (item 40). `flexible_power_kw` is the turn-up ceiling (most the plugged-in, below-target EVs could draw); turn-up headroom is that minus current home draw. The Overview hero plots normal-path flexible power as "available"; the smart path's higher line is captioned "headroom still unused while waiting for cheap slots", not extra flexibility. `price_band_shift` also reports absolute per-band home energy for each path so the Plan lens can show normal vs smart totals.
47. **Fleet cap meets the study end (lead, 28 September 2026, under Mike's "go with your recommendations").** Smart-charging plan windows stop at the end of the study week, so EVs still plugged in on Sunday evening must fit their need before midnight. Uncapped, the fleet crams that energy into the last few half-hours; with the optional fleet cap on, not all of it fits and the week closes short. In a 200-EV seed-42 run at 600 kW, about £290 of the £298 reduction in illustrative saving and every "material" week came from this closing shortfall; spreading into dearer half-hours cost only about £30. The model stays as specified (cap holds, EV charges what fits) and this is labelled as a limitation: a caption on capped runs and a Limits entry say the capped closing shortfall is mostly a study-end effect, and the Value and risk components chart shows home import cost separately from unrecovered energy value. Exempting the last evening from the cap or simulating a run-out period past the week were rejected as extra machinery for an optional, default-off setting. Within one half-hour, lower EV indices book first; this is deterministic and does not change fleet totals.
48. **Remove the fleet cap; stochastic day-ahead prices (Mike, 28 September 2026).** The optional fleet charging cap (item 45b) is removed, which also retires item 47. Herding came from one deterministic daily-cosine forecast shared by every day and world, so every smart EV picked the same trough half-hour. Each simulated week now has its own day-ahead price path: the daily cosine plus illustrative AR(1) half-hourly noise with an editable SD, so the cheapest half-hour moves from night to night and week to week. Smart charging plans on its own week's day-ahead path at plug-in (day-ahead prices are published before delivery); the realised price adds the existing independent AR(1) forecast error, and the cost effect is valued at realised prices. Within one simulated week all smart EVs still share one price signal and there is no site or network limit, so they can still bunch into that night's cheapest half-hours; this stays labelled as a finding.
49. **Sessions that run past the study end charge as normal (lead, 28 September 2026, under Mike's "go with your recommendations").** Smart-charging plan windows were cut at the end of the study week, so Sunday-evening EVs crammed their energy into the last half-hours before midnight, a spike (about 900 kW for 200 EVs) that is an artefact of the study boundary, not behaviour. A smart session whose next departure falls after the study end cannot see a full plan window, so it charges as soon as it is plugged in, like the normal path. The within-week herding finding of item 48 is measured as each simulated week's own peak, not the peak of the median band across weeks, whose timing varies.
50. **Unmanaged, not normal (Mike, 29 September 2026).** The counterfactual path (charge at full home power from plug-in until the preferred target) is labelled "Unmanaged" on every screen and in docs. "Baseline" is reserved for the trading settlement baseline (item 53, decision 0005). Internal `path_id` values stay `normal`/`selected`.
51. **Plug-out is departure; wider fat-tailed clocks (Mike, 29 September 2026).** One departure draw per EV-day sets both unplug and trip start, centred on the cohort's departure time; plug-in is an independent draw around the cohort's plug-in time. Both use a truncated Student-t (one uniform per cell through the t quantile, rounded to 30 min, clipped at ±3 h; editable scales and degrees of freedom), giving about ±2 h P5–P95. The smart plan still targets typical departure minus the margin; an early departure is leaving before the plan finishes. The default departure margin rises from 1 h to 2 h so early departures stay near 3% of sessions.
52. **Noon-to-noon study horizon and a 7-day warm-up with prices (Mike, 29 September 2026).** The study runs from London 12:00 on the start date for 7 session nights, so every overnight session sits inside it and every night is a smart-charging night; this removes the first-night artefact and the item 49 special case. Reporting days are session nights. The warm-up is 7 days (editable 3–14) and prices are generated over it, so the settlement baseline has history; warm-up days are simulated but not shown.
53. **Prices from a simple system model (Mike, 29 September 2026).** Day-ahead prices come from a synthetic system net demand (a demand shape plus a heating term driven by the sampled temperatures, minus a persistent daily wind capacity factor) passed through a convex supply curve, plus a daily level walk and the half-hour AR(1) noise; shape and curve are calibrated once from free Elexon half-hourly market index data (attributed, not a runtime dependency). Wholesale prices stay national. Home import (the tariff leg) is valued at the day-ahead price; plans see only day-ahead prices already published at plug-in (13:00 D−1). Intraday (hourly martingale updates, correlated across adjacent periods, frozen at gate closure) and imbalance (intraday close plus a two-state NIV premium plus fat tails) are generated for the trading ledger.
54. **Flexibility and screens (Mike, 29 September 2026).** "Deferrable power by slack" (kW by slack bucket, unmanaged path) replaces the time-slack chart; turn-up headroom is shown for the smart path only; a coincidence factor (peak ÷ plugged-in charger capacity) joins the herding measure. Overview gains a Key stats lens (dense P10/P50/P90 table with reference, change against unmanaged and short verdicts) and replaces its flexible-power chart with tiles; Fleet week and Flexibility merge; Drivers gains a Sessions lens (per-archetype distributions with source markers); a new Trading page. Filtering: level fixed by page, one Group selector on Drivers lenses with a group key, one shared day-type control. Charts use a flat background; on-screen copy carries no internal citations. The cohort mix becomes editable (source shares as default, illustrative override).
55. **Events, grid requests and locality (Mike, 29 September 2026).** An editable events table with presets: cold still evening known day-ahead; surprise evening spike (intraday and imbalance only); DFS turn-down called day-ahead; local turn-up in one zone. Weather and wind shocks act through net demand and the supply curve, so the same shock moves price more when the system is tight. Turn-down and turn-up requests reach the planner as a penalty or bonus inside their window for the EVs in scope; delivery is measured against the baseline and paid per MWh as an illustrative trading bucket. EVs are assigned to four illustrative network zones with optional headroom limits; local events apply to one zone. Day-ahead-notice events come first, short-notice replanning later; bidirectional (V2G) export is optional and last.
56. **Stochastic net-demand shocks (Mike, 29 September 2026).** Besides the scripted presets of item 55, net demand carries a random shock process: frequent mild shocks and about five big shocks per simulated week, in both directions (scarcity pushes prices up; surplus pushes them down, possibly negative overnight). Shocks start only between London 15:00 and 09:00 the next morning, have a trapezoid shape (mild 0.5–2 h, big 1–4 h), fat-tailed big sizes, and are either known day-ahead (they enter the day-ahead price) or surprises (intraday and imbalance only). Arrivals use a fixed number of candidates per night with uniform thinning so draw counts stay fixed. Rates, sizes, tail, up-probability, durations, window and known shares are editable illustrative assumptions. Shocks pass through the convex supply curve, so the same GW moves price more when the system is tight.
57. **Trading position uses the existing planner, not an LP (lead, 29 September 2026, under Mike's "go with your recommendations").** The contract review showed the fleet-aggregate LP pools sessions (a known day-ahead bias), has tied optima under flat prices, and adds about 16,000 solver calls per 100-week run. The aggregator's expected plan instead applies `action.plan_cheapest_slots` to the expected sessions at each decision instant: exact for this cheapest-slots-before-deadline problem, vectorised, and the same rule the chargers dispatch with. Positions forecast the settled deviation (baseline minus expected metered import); only observable turn-down (deviation > 0 inside the turn-down window) is settled; the unmet-charge penalty applies only to shortfall beyond the unmanaged path's own; grid-event windows pause fleet-wide trading in v1. Non-response (a session ignoring its plan) defaults to 5% as an illustrative behaviour assumption. No decision uses information published after its decision instant.
58. **Supplier and trader views, market realism and delivery realism (Mike, 29 September 2026).** Informed by Axle's supplier product ("provide your own price curves & adjust positions", "track revenues and costs against variable baselines", forecasts, scenario comparison) and the data-science role. (A) Net demand gains solar (capacity × daylight shape × daily cloud factor) and Edit assumptions gains a season/start-date picker. (B) Trader metrics: capture rate (full ÷ perfect foresight), value of intraday (full − day-ahead only), cost of uncertainty (perfect foresight − full), imbalance cost £/MWh and % of traded volume, firmness (delivered ÷ committed), £/MW/year of flexible capacity, day-ahead spread, open position by half-hour, P10 and CVaR5. (C) A firm-MW commitment share (default 80% of forecast availability committed day-ahead; the rest traded intraday). (D) A Supplier page: availability (turn-down/turn-up MW with P10–P90) and a flexibility cost curve (MW available above each £/MWh at a chosen half-hour); positions table with CSV download and upload of the supplier's own 48-value price curve; shape premium (load-weighted minus baseload price, unmanaged vs smart), value per household per month with its distribution, revenue by market, archetype and zone. (E) Non-response by archetype, a baseline-trap switch (baseline from already-flexed nights), an optional hold-out control group measuring baseline bias. (F) Stress presets: cold still week, sunny negative-price weekend, telemetry outage. The day-ahead price is the expected price given day-ahead information (no free money), with a deliberate premium parameter defaulting to 0. Intraday P&L in v1 is all rebalancing; re-optimisation appears only when dispatch follows the latest price.
59. **Firm-MW availability with shared factors (Mike, 29 September 2026).** Deliverable flexibility is modelled with common factors so P-values are honest: a day-level plug-in factor shared by every EV in a simulated day (about ±15%, logit scale), optional holiday/half-term presets, and illustrative charger manufacturers (editable shares and response rates) with a per-night cloud-outage probability that removes a manufacturer's whole slice; non-response comes from manufacturer response and outages. Summaries report deliverable MW per half-hour for turn-down and turn-up, held for 1, 2 and 4 hours, at two horizons (day-ahead across weeks; intraday at 17:00 given who is plugged in), with a leave-one-week-out calibration backtest (coverage, pinball loss, reliability). Commitment can be at the newsvendor quantile (price ÷ expected shortfall cost) as well as the fixed share. Also: a product sheet (MWh, event length, notice, ramp, recovery by window), blackout windows, price-weighted availability, a per-EV settlement file for a sample week, charge completion by departure, net shape change with rebound in the supplier positions, firmness by manufacturer, and a concept notebook fitting a top-down quantile model (scikit-learn gradient boosting with a conformal correction) against the bottom-up bands. Batteries, heat pumps, opt-out churn and platform SLAs are limitations.
60. **Plug-in skip share and day-to-day swing (Mike, 29 September 2026).** Option A: one shared skip-share factor applies to every clocked cohort (always-plugged excluded), p_eff = p·(1 − s), s = expit(μ_skip + σ_week·y_w + σ_day·z_wd + h_d). Defaults: median skip about 12% of cars on an ordinary night, with σ_day set so the fleet plug-in rate moves about ±15% (one SD, relative) from day to day, and a smaller week factor for estimation uncertainty. Mike accepts that this removes roughly 10–15% of sessions from every run, lowering flexibility, savings and peaks. The intraday (17:00) availability forecast covers the whole night: known plugged-in EVs plus expected not-yet-arrived sessions.
61. **Calibrating the day factor (lead, 29 September 2026, correcting the reading of item 60).** The "±15%" in Mike's source example is the day-to-day relative spread of the share of cars charging in one evening half-hour (12% ± 1.8 points), which implies a pairwise correlation of about 0.003 between cars (about 300 effectively independent cars in 10,000). Reading it as a 15% spread of the whole plug-in rate would imply a correlation of about 0.12 (about 8 effectively independent cars at any fleet size), which is far stronger than the source. σ_day is therefore calibrated so the implied correlation of evening turn-down availability is about 0.003 at defaults; the median skip share stays about 12% (Mike accepted fewer sessions), and σ_week is about a third of σ_day.

63. **Day-factor arithmetic, correcting item 61's premise (lead, 29 September 2026).** Under the multiplicative skip share every skip-driven share has the same relative day-to-day spread, and an indicator with mean π̄ gets pairwise correlation relSD²·π̄/(1−π̄). Matching Mike's source example (evening charging share 12% ± 1.8 points, correlation ≈ 0.003, about 320 effective EVs in 10,000) needs σ_day ≈ 1.0 — the same value as item 60's reading. The "about 8 effective EVs" is the same factor seen through the whole-fleet plug-in count (mean 0.84, correlation ≈ 0.12), not a stronger factor. Consequences at σ_day = 1.0: about 16% fewer sessions on average, about 27% skipping on a +1 SD night and about 50% on a +2 SD night, and firm share (P90/P50) levelling at about 0.81 as the fleet grows. σ_day is one editable record; a milder value (for example 0.5) is a one-number change awaiting Mike's confirmation.
64. **Day factor σ_day = 0.5 (Mike, 29 September 2026).** The default day-level skip factor is σ_day = 0.5 (σ_week about a third of it), milder and more plausible for plug-in behaviour alone than 1.0: about 12–13% fewer sessions on average, about 18% skipping on a +1 SD night and about 27% on a +2 SD night, evening-availability correlation about 0.0006 and firm share (P90/P50) about 0.86 at 1,000 EVs and 0.91 at 10,000. The value is one editable record; the §10.1b table keeps the alternatives. Shared timing variation (people plugging in earlier or later together) is not modelled and is listed as a limitation.

68. **Multi-day sessions (Mike, 29 September 2026; amends item 51).** A driver who does not drive on a day stays plugged in until the next trip, rather than unplugging at the day's departure draw. This gives daytime flexibility for low-mileage drivers (some charge at home while not driving) and brings plug-in gaps closer to CNZ, where about 30 % of plug-ins come two or more days after the last. The departure draw still sets the unplug time on days the EV drives; plug-in and departure clocks, the skip share and seeded draws otherwise follow item 51 and the shared-factor rules.
