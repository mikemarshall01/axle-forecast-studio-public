# How this was built

This is how I built Axle Forecast Studio: the architecture, the decisions behind it and why, and how the work was done. I cite real decision numbers, commits and file names so each choice can be checked against the code. It is current to the final model layout (commit `a975e7c` and the docs that follow it). The second full chart audit and the whole-app milestone verification have since run (see the note below).

I gave Python the model because NumPy, SciPy and statsmodels suit stock-flow simulation (tracking a stock, here battery energy, as flows move it), random sampling and time-series generation. A Streamlit and Plotly dashboard can then read and draw the results without a second application layer.

## Architecture

Python owns the physical stock-flow model, the synthetic market paths, the planning and decision logic, aggregation, and any replay or comparison. Streamlit, Plotly and Pandas consume validated results; they never recalculate physics, policy or settlement (AGENTS.md, "Model and UI boundaries"). A full Monte Carlo run starts only from an explicit **Run** click; opening the app, editing a draft, navigating and ordinary reruns never trigger one (decision 0002).

The model was nine modules in `src/axle_studio/model/`, one concern each, at this layout (decision 0004 item 15, commit `a975e7c`): `assumptions.py` (every value), `clock.py` (the UTC grid), `settings.py` (run shape types), `sampling.py` (every random draw), `physics.py` (the half-hour energy balance and world-first sums), `action.py` (price-optimised smart home charging and its cost), `forecast.py` (the one runner, `run_forecast`), `summaries.py` (everything derived after a run, and Compare) and `individual.py` (one EV's replay). Trading, events, supplier and household work has since added nine more, still one concern each: `events.py` (scripted events and shocks), `market.py` (the illustrative trading overlay), `availability.py` and `availability_backtest.py` (firm-MW deliverable kW and its calibration backtest), `product.py` (the supplier-facing product sheet and settlement file), `supplier.py` (the illustrative supplier P&L and Partners frames), `growth.py` (the Partners growth calculator), `household.py` (what one customer gets) and `replay.py` (the fleet replay). `docs/CODE_GUIDE.md` walks through all eighteen in reading order.

Two models remain (decision 0004 item 18): **no action**, where each EV charges normally when connected and below its target, and **smart charging**, which plans each home session against the forecast prices instead (item 38, which replaced an earlier import-ceiling design of the same name). Smart charging is the default. Both simulate every EV, in every world, at half-hour resolution, from persistent per-EV traits through daily trips, home connection and charging. Fleet, cohort and individual figures all come from the same per-EV, per-world state, summed within a world before any percentile is taken across worlds (decision 0001; `docs/REVIEW_POLICY.md`).

The dashboard has nine pages (How it works, Overview, Drivers, Smart charging, Trading, Supplier, Partners, Replay, Compare; the streamlining pass fixed five, decision 0004 item 28, items 54–66 later added Trading, Supplier and Replay, and Partners followed), a run bar with a model control, status chip, Edit assumptions dialog and Run button, and automatic history of the last three runs (`docs/DASHBOARD_DESIGN.md`). Every chart is Plotly, drawn through one shared template in `ui/style.py` (item 29). The final views were mounted on real results in commit `4fe22d3`, and the superseded views deleted in `e55642d`.

## The explicit model and the action model

The explicit models replace an earlier design that treated a cohort's source plug-in and plug-out clocks as if they described a whole journey away from home. I corrected this: those clocks are evidence the car was connected, not a trip's start and end. The explicit models instead sample a persistent population, then daily trips, home connections and weather per EV per world, and derive plug-in state and state of charge from battery stock-flow.

When a trip would take a battery below a top-up threshold (10% SoC by default), the EV tops up at a public charger to a target (80%) and carries on, so no EV strands (decision 0004 item 32, commit `42276d1`). The default threshold started at 20% and was lowered to 10% once the archetype script's CNZ comparison showed the gap it left (item 37, commit `3971f40`; see "Mistakes caught by review" below). Runs start settled: 80% opening SoC and a warm-up, three days at first (item 36, commit `7b4b0b4`), later widened to seven and made editable (item 52). There is one home charging power for every EV (item 34).

The action model gives each simulated week its own day-ahead price path, from a net-demand and illustrative merit-order supply-curve model with an intraday close as the realised price (`sampling.generate_market_price_paths`, decision 0004 items 48, 53, 55, 56), and a smart charger that plans each home session at plug-in: knowing the EV's stock, the cohort's typical departure time minus a safety margin, and that week's day-ahead path, it fills the cheapest day-ahead half-hours up to home charging power (`action.plan_cheapest_slots`, decision 0004 item 38, which replaced an earlier 0 kW ceiling in the 18:00 London half-hour and its per-EV eligibility screen). It limits and times home import; it never forces it, and an EV that leaves earlier than expected simply misses the rest of its planned charge; a session whose next departure falls after the study end charges as normal instead (item 49). Cost effects (home import, public charging, unrecovered energy, unserved travel) are shown as separate illustrative components, never a settled amount (decision 0003).

## Key decisions and why

- **Explicit per-EV stock-flow, and removing the superseded modes** (decision 0004 item 18). The cohort, compact and synthetic six-route models used connection-clock timing as a stand-in for travel, which M06 in `docs/USER_FEEDBACK.md` identifies as physically wrong. They were removed with their views, tests and configs (commits `d505c20`, `57f87bb`, `ce718a3`).
- **Per-EV eligibility for the wholesale action, later superseded** (item 8, then item 38). Screening the whole fleet together first rejected the action at 1,000 EVs because some EV always failed; screening EV by EV and day by day fixed that. I then replaced the ceiling-and-eligibility design outright with price-optimised smart charging (item 38, commit `1b693a8`): every EV plans its own session against the forecast, so there is no fleet-wide or per-EV screen left to fail.
- **Illustrative pricing, always labelled** (decision 0003, item 4). Public charging is priced at an editable £0.79/kWh, not a tariff claim, and no "Axle cash" total is shown.
- **Fixed UTC horizon across a clock change** (item 1). The study is always 336 UTC half-hours, so a clock change does not silently shorten or lengthen it; it later moved from London midnight to London noon on the start date (item 52), so a noon-to-noon block holds a whole overnight session instead of cutting the first night in half.
- **One assumptions module** (items 15 and 21). `model/assumptions.py` holds every value with unit, evidence kind, source and meaning, and the Edit assumptions dialog is generated from it (commits `026144f`, `ad8240d`). The JSON configs and loaders are gone.
- **scipy.stats and statsmodels for sampling** (item 14). Independent draws use named `scipy.stats` distributions on the run's one `np.random.default_rng(seed)`; price noise is an AR(1) from `statsmodels.tsa.arima_process.ArmaProcess` (commits `160cfc9`, `8c8c234`). Each channel takes a fixed number of draws, so editing one assumption leaves every other channel unchanged and Compare keeps matched futures.
- **No stranding** (item 32, superseding items 3 and 31). Threshold top-ups replaced "charge at the destination only if the return would fail" and the stranding rules, because a stranded EV made the rest of its week meaningless.
- **Top-up threshold lowered to 10%** (item 37, commit `3971f40`). The 20% default left no plug-in below 20% SoC while CNZ observed about 3% below 10%; 10% narrows that gap by letting plug-ins between 10% and 20% happen, closer to CNZ's "fewer than 10% below 20%" upper bound. It stays editable, bounds 5–50%.
- **Compare from slim run records** (item 35). Run history keeps only the frames Compare needs for every kept run, so the latest run alone holds a full result.
- **The five-page streamlined design** (item 28), since grown to nine pages as Trading, Supplier, Partners and Replay were added (items 54–66; `docs/DASHBOARD_DESIGN.md` section 2). An interviewer with ten minutes sees one question and one hero chart per page, and I can edit an assumption and rerun without leaving it.

## How the work was done

I designed the model myself: its structure and architecture, the inputs, parameters and variables, and the actors in it (drivers, the smart charger, the trading desk, the supplier, households). The agents built it to my design, under my direction, and I made every decision along the way. I did the work with AI coding agents: a lead session held the accepted decisions and the shared contracts, and released one coherent task at a time to writer, reviewer and verifier agents working in isolated Git worktrees with exclusive, non-overlapping file ownership (AGENTS.md, "Migration and collaboration"). A writer implemented its task, ran focused checks, and made a local commit of only its own files. A fresh, independent reviewer with no memory of the writer's reasoning then checked that frozen commit against the accepted contracts, `docs/REVIEW_POLICY.md` and the surrounding code before it was merged. I worked directly with the agents on the decisions: I set the requirements, made or approved each modelling and design decision, recorded in `docs/decisions` and the review log, and checked the physics and results myself.

## What I designed

I specified how each part of the model works, as the list below says; the agents wrote the code to that specification, and I checked the result against the code.

- **The fleet and its six archetypes.** Six driver types, in the population shares from Axle's own sheet (40/30/10/10/9/1%), each with its own battery size and typical annual mileage (with a per-driver random multiplier round that typical figure), how often it plugs in, and its usual home arrival and departure clock hours. Those clock hours vary night to night around the typical time using a fat-tailed (Student-t) spread, because real plug-in and plug-out times have more early and late outliers than a bell curve allows.
- **Trips and departures.** Every EV's day is its own simulated trip, or a day it stays home, each with its own distance, dwell time away and driving speed. Departure is also the moment it unplugs, so a trip and a plug-out are the same event, not two separately guessed ones.
- **Plug-ins.** Whether an EV plugs in on a given night is its own random draw around the archetype's usual habit, but nights and weeks also share some randomness: a shared day factor and a shared week factor move every EV's plug-in chance together, so a quiet night or a quiet week happens to the whole fleet at once, the way real weather or events would. A plug-in session can run for more than one night if the EV stays parked at home.
- **Home charging power and efficiency.** One charging power figure for every EV's home charger, and a charging efficiency that converts grid electricity into battery energy, so the model never puts more into the battery than the grid actually supplied.
- **The battery physics.** Every EV's battery is tracked half-hour by half-hour: energy in from home or public charging, energy out from driving, always between 0% and 100% state of charge and always balanced. If a trip would take the battery below a minimum threshold, the car tops up at a public charger to a target level first, so no trip is ever missed and no car strands.
- **Weather.** Each simulated day has its own temperature, and driving efficiency (miles per battery kWh) falls the further that temperature sits from a mild reference point in either direction, the way real cars behave in cold or very hot weather.
- **Prices.** Each simulated week gets its own made-up wholesale power price: a demand pattern by time of day, season and weekday/weekend, plus weather, wind and solar effects and random shocks, feeding an illustrative supply curve partly calibrated to real GB price behaviour (Elexon data), never real market data itself. Day-ahead prices are published once a day at a fixed London time, and the model builds separate day-ahead, intraday and imbalance prices for different uses.
- **The smart charger's plan.** At each plug-in, the charger works out the cheapest half-hours between then and when the car usually leaves, minus a safety margin, using only the prices already published at that moment, and fills the battery in those cheapest slots first.
- **Intraday dispatch.** A fixed share of cars lock in their overnight plan and do not change it; the rest re-check the price every hour and only switch plan if the saving clears a threshold, so plans do not flip on noise.
- **The trading desk.** An aggregator sells turn-down against a rolling baseline in the style of a real GB balancing-market rule (BL01), under three different trading strategies, with every trade, cost and settlement written to a per-night ledger.
- **The supplier's P&L.** What smart charging is worth to an energy supplier: procurement cost, hedging error, grid-event income and customer payments, each shown as its own line, never netted into one figure unless the terms for that net are actually set.
- **Households.** What one customer gets from smart charging: their own savings, readiness for the next trip and CO2 shifted, read straight from the same run rather than recalculated.
- **Firm MW.** How much guaranteed turn-down or turn-up the fleet could promise a grid operator, in a product sheet of windows and durations, allowing for chargers that respond late, chargers that do not respond at all, and each maker's own cloud-outage risk, with a backtest that holds out one simulated week at a time to check the forecast was honest about its own uncertainty.
- **Many simulated weeks, fairly compared.** Every result comes from many separate simulated weeks, each with its own random trips, plug-ins, weather and prices. The same random numbers are reused across a change so two runs differ only in the thing that changed, and every total is added up inside one simulated week before any percentile is taken across weeks, never the other way round.

## Checks

Every writer ran focused `ruff check`, `ruff format --check` and `pytest` on its own files before handing off, and the lead reran affected and then full checks after each integration. On the final layout (`2f53ee4`, with this docs pass's in-app text edits) the full suite passed **631 tests** in about 3 minutes. The count is lower than an earlier 1,221 because the superseded models and views were removed with their tests.

Other checks ran through the build. **Golden comparisons** recomputed chart values independently of the rendering code: the first chart audit (`docs/reviews/2026-09-28-chart-audit.md`) found fleet bands equal to a groupby-quantile over worlds on all 336 slots, and per-EV replays summing to the fleet total within 5e-14. The kernel is checked against a slow scalar oracle (`tests/fixtures/scalar_kernel_oracle.py`). A **gap check** (task G1) reviewed the completion plan against the decisions and the brief before work started (commit `3a9a549`). `tests/model/test_assumption_effects.py` checks that each editable assumption moves the outputs in the expected direction. Both notebooks were re-executed on the project kernel against the final API (`ca5b389`), and `scripts/run_archetypes.py` runs the archetypes on `run_forecast` (`beaf0ed`).

The second full chart audit (task A2: every final chart at 1440 and 390 px, contrast, notebooks and script output) has since run: `docs/reviews/2026-09-28-chart-audit-2.md` recomputed every plotted value in the final app independently of the rendering code (232 of 232 matched, largest difference 1.8e-12) across both models, both notebooks and the archetype script, confirmed every first-audit blocking finding resolved, and filed nine presentation findings of its own (B1-B9) plus optional polish items.

The `docs/VERIFY_APP.md` whole-app milestone verification ran against commit `d65c534`. Every user-journey check passed at 1440 and 390 px (a 1,000 EVs × 100 weeks run took about 63 s and 1.2 GB, at that five-page layout); the one failure was an unformatted notebook cell, fixed in `930e202`. Its smaller layout and wording findings were fixed with the second goal review. Trading, Supplier and Replay have since added their own physics reads to every run, so the run time today is longer than this historical figure; the run bar shows the actual time.

## Mistakes caught by review, and what changed

Independent review caught real defects before they reached the app:

- **Unpriced unserved travel would show a false saving.** If the action path leaves an EV with less charge and a missed trip, and that trip is not priced, the energy just stays in the battery and the action looks cheaper. Item 13 values unserved travel at the public rate (commit `9f2aa55`).
- **A fleet-wide eligibility check rejected the action outright.** One ineligible EV could veto the action for everyone. Item 8 and commit `8cb8537` screen each EV instead.
- **Stranding hid missed travel, then went away.** A stranded EV's later trips silently vanished from unserved travel, so item 31 counted them (commit `a2a1b25`). I then replaced stranding altogether with threshold top-ups (item 32, `42276d1`). Follow-up review found the dead stranding logic and a per-EV mismatch between public import and top-up energy (`0310caf`), and a replay that did not receive the top-up threshold and target (`7be7798`).
- **Lower charging power raised home import.** Checking each assumption's effect found that cutting home charging power from 7.0 to 3.6 kW raised study-week home import by about 190 kWh. The cause was a start-up artefact: a 55% opening SoC and one warm-up day meant the refill spilled into the reported week. The fix is the settled start (item 36, `1b4eea9`, `7b4b0b4`), and a test now checks the direction.
- **The top-up threshold removed low plug-ins.** The archetype script's CNZ comparison showed zero plug-ins below 10% or 20% SoC, against CNZ's roughly 3% below 10%. The cause is the 20% top-up threshold: no EV reaches home below it. The script now says so (`78cb44a`) and it is listed as a known limitation rather than tuned away. I then lowered the default threshold to 10% (item 37, `3971f40`): plug-ins below 10% still cannot occur by construction, but plug-ins between 10% and 20% now can, narrowing rather than closing the gap.
- **Compare crashed on runs it could not pair.** `compare_runs` correctly refuses runs with a different world count or horizon, but the page let the error through. The page now explains why the runs cannot be paired, and names a model change first (`fe8cb50`).
- **Validation gaps.** A personal mileage CV above about 1e154 overflowed to zero mileage; CVs above 10 are now rejected (`7a98188`). A top-up target at or below the threshold could reach the model and fail the run; `assumptions.validation_errors` now rejects it in the dialog (`ad8240d`). In the other direction, checks that only re-validated values the model had built itself were removed (`19cf427`), keeping validation at the external boundary.
- **The horizon needed an anchor across a clock change** (item 1, commits `09a15a9`, `1ee5daf`), and **a shared virtual environment silently re-pointed itself** when a worktree ran `uv sync`; workers now always pass `--no-sync` and `PYTHONPATH=src` (`8915b63`).
- **The 18:00 ceiling and its eligibility screen were the wrong shape for the question.** They answered "should Axle defer one half-hour", not "how much charging can be moved, when". I replaced them outright with price-optimised smart charging (item 38, commit `1b693a8`): every EV plans its own session against the forecast and its expected departure, so the model now answers the flexibility question the brief actually asks.
- **Fleet week's across-simulated-weeks band told the reader nothing.** A 1,000-EV fleet average moves under one point between weeks (plugged-in P90−P10 median 0.8 pp, SoC 0.6 pp), so the band was there but invisible against the line. Item 39 adds a per-half-hour across-EV band alongside it (`fleet_interval_ev_bands`, commit `e19f696`); Fleet week now shows both spreads (goal review item 16 later narrowed the across-EV band to Battery SoC, where it is informative, and dropped it elsewhere).
- **Smart charging herds the fleet onto one cheap half-hour.** The goal-level review (`docs/reviews/2026-09-28-goal-review.md`) found that because every EV ranked one deterministic forecast curve shared by every day and world, in a 200-EV run before the weekday/weekend windows were added, about 140 EVs charged together at 05:30, a roughly 982 kW spike against a 537 kW normal evening peak. There is no site or network limit to stop it. Item 45 first records this as a labelled finding; item 48 later gives each simulated week its own day-ahead price path instead of adding the fleet-wide charging cap item 45 proposed, so the cheapest half-hour now moves from week to week, herding within a week remains and stays labelled as a finding, and a later numbers-refresh pass re-measures the spike under the current stochastic-price model.
- **"9 of 10 weeks not recovered" was a false alarm.** The flag is fleet-wide and binary, and fired because one EV missed one half-hour of charge against a median saving of over a thousand pounds. Item 45 replaces it with a magnitude (`not_recovered_summary`: unrecovered kWh as a share of weekly import, and sessions affected) and a material threshold, default 1% of weekly home import.
- **An invented SoC-dependent plug-in trigger did not survive a source check.** Item 41 proposed that EVs plug in more readily when they arrive low on charge, to make plug-in behaviour more realistic. Checking the CNZ report properly found no evidence for that: plug-in there reads as habitual, not SoC-triggered. Item 42 dropped the invented trigger and kept the archetype sheet's supplied daily probabilities, adding only the weekday/weekend home-connection windows the report does support (CNZ p.11 Fig.4, p.12 Fig.5), now implemented in `sampling.sample_connection_opportunities`.
- **Smart charging crammed last-evening sessions into the study's last half-hours.** Cutting every plan window at the study end left EVs plugged in on the study's last evening no full window to plan against, so they crowded their remaining need into the last half-hours before the study end, a boundary artefact rather than behaviour. Item 49 has a session whose next expected departure falls after the study end charge as soon as it is plugged in instead, like the normal path.

## What is still illustrative

Everything commercial is illustrative: the £0.79/kWh public rate, each simulated week's synthetic day-ahead, intraday and imbalance price paths and the cost components. None is a real tariff, market forecast, settlement figure or Axle cash (decision 0003). The six archetypes' shares, mileage and physical specifications are source data; the coefficients of variation, timing spread, weather sensitivity and top-up threshold around them are editable illustrative assumptions, not calibrated. DFS turn-down and local turn-up requests, and supplier optimisation, were cut here and have since been built (scripted events, item 55; the Supplier page, items 58 and 62). Frequency response, capacity market and V2G export remain out of scope, and the How it works page says so plainly.
