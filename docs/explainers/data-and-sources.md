# Data and sources

This is what every number in this app is built from: what came from a real document or a real dataset, what is a modelling choice I made and state, and what the model generates itself. This page is the catalogue; it does not repeat the calibration working (`docs/research/elexon-price-calibration.md`) or the cell-by-cell crosswalk (`docs/sources/cohort-crosswalk.md`), it points to them. `docs/CODE_GUIDE.md` gives the reading order through the code; `how-the-forecast-works.md` gives the method.

## 1. Evidence classes

Every value the model uses is a record in `model/assumptions.py` with one of three evidence tokens, matched by the app's own badges (`docs/DASHBOARD_DESIGN.md` section 3.5):

| Token | Badge | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `source` | **Source (CNZ/Axle sheet)**, grey | Taken directly from a real document: a workbook cell, or plain arithmetic on one. Read-only in Edit assumptions. |
| `illustrative` | **Illustrative assumption**, orange | A stated modelling choice with no source cell: a shape, a rate, an engineering constant, or a source-traced default the dialog lets you edit (home charging power, the cohort mix). Editable, and labelled illustrative wherever it appears. |
| `synthetic` | **Synthetic**, violet | Generated by the model itself: the price paths, the weather draws, the random shocks. Nothing here is observed. |

A fourth badge, **Model output**, carries no evidence token: it is a number the run produced (a chart series, a KPI), not an input.

`model/assumptions.py`'s own module docstring explains the mapping in more detail: the underlying source crosswalk (`docs/sources/cohort-crosswalk.md`) uses a finer five-way classification (`supplied`, `derived`, `assumed`, `technical`, `synthetic`), and this module collapses `supplied`/`derived` onto `source` and `assumed`/`technical` onto `illustrative`, keeping the finer kind as a prefix in the record's `source` text (for example `"technical model constant; ..."`) wherever it would otherwise be lost.

## 2. Real sources used

Three real documents or datasets feed the model. Nothing else in the app is sourced: everything else is a stated illustrative choice or a synthetic generator.

### 2.1 Axle's supplied archetype workbook

The six driver archetypes (Average UK, Intelligent Octopus average, Infrequent charging, Infrequent driving, Scheduled charging, Always plugged-in) and their population share, annual mileage, battery size, efficiency, plug-in frequency, home charger power, plug-in/plug-out clock times and target SoC come directly from the workbook supplied with the Axle brief (`'Source archetypes'!A6:P11` of the preserved `Source_Evidence_Workbook.xlsx`, itself a copy of `Copy of Axle Take Home Test: Archetypes.xlsx`). Every cell used is traced, with its exact relationship to the executable value, in `docs/sources/cohort-crosswalk.md`. These values are `source` evidence and feed the simulation directly: they set the population the model draws from.

The same workbook also supplies a second, smaller set of records read by `model/assumptions.py`'s `SESSION_SOURCE_REFERENCES`: each archetype's source plug-in SoC (`'Source archetypes'!N6:N11`) and battery energy per plug-in (`'Source archetypes'!M6:M11`). These are `source` evidence too, but they are comparison markers only: shown on Drivers ▸ Sessions and the archetype table against what the model produces, and never fed into the simulation itself.

The original workbook stays outside this repository (`docs/sources/cohort-crosswalk.md` records its local path and SHA-256 digest for the migration record); its licence and deployment permission were not independently established by that crosswalk.

### 2.2 CNZ, "Learning from Intelligent Octopus" (May 2022)

A published report (cited throughout the code and decisions only as "CNZ"; this repository does not itself spell out what the acronym stands for, so treat that expansion as unverified here) supplies comparison context for plug-in behaviour, and two illustrative adaptations:

- **Comparison markers, `source` evidence, shown but never fed into the simulation** (`model/assumptions.py`'s `CNZ_CONTEXT`, decision 0004 item 42): median plug-in SoC 52%, 3% of plug-ins below 10% SoC, fewer than 10% below 20% SoC (p.13), and a weekday modal plug-in time around 18:00 (p.11). These appear as dashed reference lines and captions on Drivers ▸ Plug-ins, Sessions and Archetypes; the model's own plug-in SoC and timing are reported honestly against them, not tuned to match (decision 0004 item 42 is explicit that the gap is stated, not closed).
- **Illustrative adaptations, `illustrative` evidence, do feed the simulation**: separate weekday and weekend home-connection windows for the Average UK and Intelligent Octopus archetypes (p.11 Fig.4, p.12 Fig.5: weekday arrival about 18:00 and departure 07:00–08:00; weekend arrival about 17:00 and departure 09:00–11:00, wider spread), and a shortened weekend destination dwell for the same two archetypes (an illustrative adaptation of the same weekend timing, decision 0004 item 44). Both are labelled as adaptations of the report's figures, not as figures the report states directly, because turning a chart into a clock-time window and a dwell duration is a modelling choice.
- The report also gives corroborating, not primary, evidence for the six archetypes' plug-in probabilities: CNZ's Table 2 (p.11) and p.19–20 report that most vehicles plug in daily, consistent with (but not the source of) the workbook's own supplied 1.0/day (0.2/day for infrequent charging) rates. CNZ gives no evidence that plug-in propensity depends on state of charge, so the model adds no SoC-dependent plug-in trigger (decision 0004 item 42).

### 2.3 Elexon BMRS market data (calibration only, never live in the app)

The synthetic day-ahead price generator's intraday shape (the weekday/weekend × winter/summer harmonic curves), its day-to-day level persistence, its half-hour noise, and the intraday/imbalance premium were calibrated once against twelve months of free, public GB balancing data from the Elexon Insights Solution API (`data.elexon.co.uk/bmrs`): the half-hourly market index (`balancing/pricing/market-index`, provider `APXMIDP`) and the system (imbalance) prices (`balancing/settlement/system-prices/{date}`). The fetch (`scripts/fetch_elexon_prices.py`) and the fit (`scripts/fit_price_shape.py`) are offline, one-off developer scripts; the fitted coefficients are pasted into `model/assumptions.py` by hand, and **the running app never calls Elexon or any other live data source**. The full method, date range, fitted coefficients and limitations (a two-period first-day shortfall, clock-change days folded into one bucket, one year of one market regime, the APXMIDP provider only) are in `docs/research/elexon-price-calibration.md`.

Every price shape record built from this fit carries the required licence line in its `source` field (`model/assumptions.py`, `_ELEXON_SOURCE`):

> Contains BMRS data © Elexon Limited copyright and database right 2026.

This is calibration evidence for a synthetic generator, not settlement data reproduced in the app: no price the app shows is an Elexon price, and no £ figure anywhere in the app is a market price, a bid or a settlement (decisions 0003, 0005 and 0006).

## 3. Everything else: illustrative or synthetic

Every other value is either an `illustrative` modelling choice I state (visible, editable and bounded in Edit assumptions where it can be) or `synthetic` output the model generates itself. That covers the behavioural spreads (coefficients of variation) and timing spreads, the departure margin, the public charge rate, weather, the price generator's own random paths, scripted and random price shocks, the four illustrative network zones, the four illustrative charger manufacturers and their response and outage rates, the day-level plug-in skip factor, and every trading, supplier and household £ figure. None of it is fitted to observed data. `how-the-forecast-works.md` explains each mechanism in plain words with a pointer to its concept notebook; How it works ▸ Assumptions lists every record with its evidence class, source and meaning, filterable by class.

## 4. The one limitation that applies everywhere

**Every accuracy, reliability or calibration check in this app is checked against the model's own simulated weeks, never against real fleet telemetry.** In plain terms:

- The archetypes' comparison against CNZ (section 2.2) is a population-level report figure against the model's simulated population, not a per-EV or per-fleet ground truth.
- The Elexon calibration (section 2.3) fits the *shape* of a synthetic generator to one year of real market data; once generated, a run's own price paths are never checked against what actually happened in any specific real week.
- The firm-MW calibration backtest (`model/availability_backtest.py`, decision 0004 item 59, trading contract v1 §10.3) is a leave-one-week-out check: each simulated week is held out and scored against a forecast built from the *other simulated weeks of the same run*. It reports whether the model's own forecast is honest about its own simulated spread; it says nothing about whether that spread matches a real fleet's.
- No component of the trading, supplier or household £ figures has been checked against a real settlement, a real hedge or a real customer's bill.

Nothing in this app has been validated against observed EV telemetry, a real supplier's book, or a real DNO's network. `how-the-forecast-works.md` section 9 and How it works ▸ Limits list this alongside the model's other stated limitations.
